## One-sentence version (for slides)

Kenya's carbon credit market lacks a transparent, tamper-proof way to track credits from issuance to retirement, which allows double-counting and leaves communities unable to verify their share of the revenue.

## Full version

Kenya has emerged as one of Africa's leading suppliers of voluntary carbon credits, generated by projects such as clean cookstoves, forest conservation and rangeland management [cite]. However, the voluntary carbon market suffers from double-counting, limited transparency and high intermediary fees, which undermine trust in corporate climate action (Tlili et al., 2025). Recent investigations have questioned whether many issued credits represent real emission reductions [cite: The Guardian / Die Zeit / SourceMaterial, 2023], and Kenyan projects in particular have faced criticism over how revenues are shared with local communities [cite: Survival International, 2023].

These problems persist because credit records are held in fragmented, centrally controlled registries that buyers, regulators and communities cannot independently verify. As a result, the same credit may be claimed more than once, buyers cannot trace a credit from its origin to its retirement, and communities cannot confirm that they receive the benefit share required under the Climate Change (Amendment) Act, 2023 [cite].

This project proposes **KaboniChain**, a blockchain-based platform that tokenizes verified carbon credits, records their full lifecycle on a public ledger, permanently burns credits upon retirement to prevent double-counting, and automatically pays a share of every sale to community wallets through smart contracts. This directly supports **SDG 13 (Climate Action)**, in particular **Target 13.2** (integrate climate change measures into national policies, strategies and planning), by strengthening the integrity of climate finance.

## Objectives

1. Tokenize verified carbon credits so that 1 token = 1 tonne of CO₂e.
2. Prevent double-counting by burning tokens when credits are retired.
3. Provide a public registry so that anyone can trace a credit from project to retirement.
4. Pay communities their share automatically at the moment of sale.
5. Restrict credit issuance to independent verifiers.
